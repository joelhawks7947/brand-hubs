Five rules that cover most decisions

  1. Crimson leads. Navy supports.Classic Crimson is the colour people should remember. Oxford Navy carries body text, footers and formal documents.
  2. Give it room.White and Parchment Cream should cover most of any layout, so the crimson has something to stand against.
  3. Crimson and Parchment only meet at heading size.That applies both ways round: crimson text on Parchment, and Parchment text on crimson. For small text on crimson, use White.
  4. Libre Baskerville for titles. Open Sans for everything else.The serif is for titles, headings, quotes and captions. Body copy, labels and buttons are Open Sans.
  5. Use the logo as supplied.Choose a colourway from this page. Do not recolour, stretch, outline or redraw it.

Room and size

The draft sets no clear space or minimum size. They should be rechecked once the vector artwork arrives.

Clear spaceKeep a margin on every side equal to the height of the letters in FOUNDATION. For the seal, use a quarter of its diameter.
Minimum sizeSeal: 96 px on screen, 25 mm in print, so the ring text stays readable. Wordmark: 160 px or 40 mm wide.
Too small for the seal?Use the hawk on its own, as in a browser tab icon or an app avatar at small sizes.

Two typefaces, eight roles

Aa

Libre Baskerville

Primary. Titles, headings, quotes, captions and the logo signature. It projects tradition, authority and philanthropic credibility.

Email and Office fallback: Georgia

Aa

Open Sans

Secondary. Body copy, subtitles, section headers, labels and buttons. Chosen for readability across reports and web.
